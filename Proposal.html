Leads Scraper: Multi-Industry Lead Capture and Outreach Automation

What has been built, how a lead moves from capture to a conversation, what it costs to run in the US market, and what remains.

1. Executive summary

The original system found supplier businesses for one purpose (dropshipping) and contacted them by email, SMS and AI voice call. It has been rebuilt into a platform that can serve any industry: real estate, HVAC, AI automation services, or anything that sells to local businesses. The industry is chosen from the admin panel, and nothing industry-specific remains in the code.

What changed:

  1. Industry Profiles. Search wording, scoring rules, offer, FAQ and follow-up sequence are saved as data. The original dropship setup is the first profile and scores leads exactly as before (checked against every combination of its rules).
  2. An Outreach Queue. Hot and Warm leads join it automatically. Each lead follows its profile's sequence on its own clock, inside its own working hours (its time zone), with gaps and daily limits that you set.
  3. Email through Gmail and WhatsApp through a Chrome extension. This removes the paid email platform and all SMS cost.
  4. Messages written by AI, approved by a person. Each channel is Manual, Assisted or Auto. Nothing is sent without approval until a channel is switched to Auto.
  5. Replies are understood. Replies are read, labelled, answered with a draft, and the lead's sequence stops. "STOP" or a refusal adds the lead to the do-not-contact list.
  6. Connection status on every page. The admin panel shows whether WhatsApp Web is open and the email account works, warns when messages are waiting, and offers an Open WhatsApp Web button.
Not built yet The voice agent and closing agent are deliberately last. The database is still SQLite; the move to Supabase is planned. A lead-capture key (Google Places) has not been added yet, so a first live batch has not been run.
Headline cost (US market, estimate) Running cost is roughly $0 to $60 per month for 1,000 leads captured per month: capture, email and WhatsApp are free or nearly free, the AI costs a few dollars, and the only fixed cost is the database plan if Supabase Pro is chosen. Voice calls, when added, are the one real variable cost. Details are in section 9.

3. Multi-industry design

An Industry Profile is a saved set of settings chosen when a campaign starts:

  • Search wording, for example "HVAC contractor {city}".
  • Scoring rules, each worth points, in six kinds: a stored value (rating, reviews), keywords on the website, a pattern on the website, a yes/no question answered by AI, a built-in check, or the lead's behaviour after outreach (opened, replied, call connected). Penalties are negative points.
  • Offer: what we sell, tone, language, a booking link and a list of questions and answers the AI may use.
  • Follow-up sequence: the steps, the wait between them and which leads get each step.

Profiles can be created, edited, copied, archived, exported and imported from the admin panel. Leads keep the profile they were captured under, and scores record which profile version produced them. Data that differs per lead (what the website said, what the AI answered, CSV columns) is stored as flexible fields, so a new kind of client needs no new database column.

Scoring parity. The original dropshipping rules are expressed as the first profile, and an automated test compares it with the old fixed formula for 2,304 combinations of inputs. Points, tier and the "score incomplete" flag all match.

What limits a new industry is its data source. Google Places and SerpAPI suit local businesses. Companies or individual professionals need a CSV import or another data source.

2 EnrichCrawl the site, run the profile's rules.
3 ScoreHot, Warm or Cold, with the reasons.
4 QueueHot and Warm leads join automatically.
5 WriteAI writes each due step.
6 ApproveYou approve, or the channel is Auto.
7 SendAt a planned time inside the lead's hours.

Each lead has its own clock

  • A step's wait is counted from when the previous step was actually done for that lead, not from when leads were collected. Leads captured on different days follow different schedules.
  • The due time is moved into the lead's own working hours in its own time zone (for example 09:00 to 17:00 on weekdays in Miami or Los Angeles).
  • Between two sends on a channel there is a random gap inside the range you set, and each channel has a daily limit. Follow-ups are served before first contacts.
  • A step the lead cannot receive (no email, wrong tier) is skipped and logged. A lead with only a phone number starts at the WhatsApp step.
  • A reply, an opt-out or a bounce stops the sequence.

5. Automation and approval

Each channel has a mode, set on the Outreach queue page under Sending rules:

ModeWhat happens when a step is due
ManualThe system only shows it is due. You do it yourself and press Done.
Assisted (default)The AI writes the message and it waits for your approval. You can edit it, have it rewritten, or skip the step.
AutoThe message is approved by itself and sent at its planned time, within the limits.

Preparing the data (capture, enrichment, scoring, queueing) is always automatic. Sending starts in Assisted mode, and the supervisor decides when each channel moves to Auto after the pilot: no bans or spam problems, replies read correctly, opt-outs tested, limits and cost tracking working. A separate switch decides whether the AI's answers to replies may also go out without approval; it is off by default.

Good delivery needs a separate sending account or domain, a warm-up of one to two weeks at a low volume, and a daily limit well below Gmail's own (the default is 40). Every email ends with a line asking the lead to reply STOP if it is not relevant, and carries a List-Unsubscribe header. Bounces mark the address invalid.

Open tracking is optional. It adds an invisible image to each email, so PUBLIC_BASE_URL must be an address that the lead's mail app can open (your domain, or a tunnel while testing), and EMAIL_OPEN_TRACKING must be switched on. An "open" is a hint, not proof, because some mail apps load images in advance and some never do. Loads within seconds of sending are ignored.

6.2 WhatsApp: a Chrome extension

  • The extension runs inside your own WhatsApp Web. It asks the backend what to send, sends it with a short pause, and reports sent, failed or not on WhatsApp. When a lead writes back it tells the backend.
  • The backend decides what, when, how many and whether approval is needed. The extension only delivers.
  • Chrome must be open with a WhatsApp Web tab (it can be pinned or minimised). The admin panel shows a WhatsApp chip, warns when messages are waiting and the tab is not open, and has an Open WhatsApp Web button. The extension can also open the tab by itself when messages are waiting.
  • If the tab is closed nothing is lost: messages wait and go out when it opens again.
  • Automated messaging to people who have not asked for it can get a number restricted or banned. Start with a dedicated number, low limits and the gaps from Sending rules.
  • The official WhatsApp Business API is not suitable for cold first contact: it requires the recipient's prior opt-in, and per-message fees apply.

SMS is no longer used.

6.3 Voice calls (later)

Calls need a phone number. Three ways work with Twilio: register an existing US number as a Verified Caller ID (outbound calls only), buy a Twilio number (outbound and inbound), or connect an existing carrier through a SIP trunk. A Twilio trial account can only call verified numbers, so it must be upgraded before calling real leads. The existing voice agent can be reused; the planned saving is to replace paid speech recognition and the paid voice with cheaper options after listening tests.

7. Replies and the closing agent

7.1 What happens when a lead answers

The AI reads it asWhat the system does
InterestedDrafts an answer that explains the offer briefly and invites a short call, using the profile's booking link. The lead's sequence stops.
Has a questionDrafts an answer using only the profile's offer and FAQ. If the answer is not there it says it will confirm.
Not nowDrafts a short, polite acknowledgement and brings the lead back into the sequence after a set number of days (default 45). Replies from before that no longer stop it.
StopAdds the lead's email, phone and domain to the do-not-contact list. Nothing more is sent.
OtherAutomatic replies, wrong person, unclear: nothing is sent.

Answer drafts appear in the same approval list, with the lead's message shown above. A channel in Manual mode gets no draft, because you reply yourself. Out-of-office replies are ignored. A plain "STOP" is always honoured, even if the AI is unavailable.

7.2 Closing agent (later)

Level 1: qualify and book

A voice agent confirms interest, asks a few qualifying questions, answers common questions and books a meeting in the team calendar, then writes a summary to the CRM. A person closes the deal in the meeting.

Recommended.

Level 2: full closing

The agent states pricing and agrees terms on the call.

Not recommended yet. It may promise things incorrectly and larger deals usually need a person.

8. Safety and compliance

  • Nothing is sent without approval until a channel is switched to Auto.
  • Do-not-contact list checked for every lead; email, phone and website domain are blocked together. Opt-out keywords, a bare "STOP", or an AI-read refusal all add a lead.
  • Working hours in the lead's own time zone, random gaps, and daily limits per channel.
  • Visible status: a warning appears if messages are waiting and the channel cannot send them.
  • No login: the panel and its API are open to anyone who can reach the server, so it must run on your own computer or a private network. If it is ever put on the internet, a login must be added first.
  • Legal: in the US, automated and AI-voice calls and texts are regulated (TCPA, FCC rules) and cold email must follow CAN-SPAM. The software provides the controls above; the business remains responsible for lawful use. Legal review of the scripts is recommended before the pilot.

12. What is needed

  1. A Google Places key (billing account with a budget alert) so that leads can be captured.
  2. A Gmail account for sending, with an App Password and IMAP on; ideally a separate one from the main business address.
  3. A dedicated WhatsApp number, and a computer that can keep Chrome open during sending hours.
  4. For open tracking: a public address for the backend and the tracking switch turned on (optional).
  5. Approval of the Supabase plan: Free for the pilot, Pro afterwards.
  6. The first industry and city for the pilot, and the scoring rules agreed for it.
  7. A legal review of the scripts before the pilot.
